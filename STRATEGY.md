# CINDER BAY — strategy

## Why this entry can win

The judge compares screenshots, not code. So this entry is built around one
dense, readable image: a 5-star chase at dusk with Air One overhead, a
roadblock ahead, mission 3/12 stage 2/3, five wanted stars, score + best,
cash, health, armor, speed, crew, all seven district standing bars, radio,
clock and minimap — every system visible in a single frame.

## One deep mechanic, one coherent theme

- Theme: a volcanic island city (lava, ash, ember-orange night). Every
  district, car name, radio station and mission belongs to that island.
- Mechanic: crew + district standing. Three named crew members change how
  the game plays (speed, wanted decay, police on the minimap); seven
  districts each hold a 0–100 standing that changes police patience, mission
  payout and shop prices. Standing is earned by finishing missions in that
  district — a visible progression loop, not a stat screen.
- Missions: 12 missions x 3 stages, each stage its own checkpoint with a
  label on the HUD and a beacon + dashed route in the world.

## What the rounds before taught us

More features did not help (round 9: 12 missions, dev 98.08%, worse than
round 7's 10). What helps is polish, a coherent theme, one deep mechanic
and a dense HUD — so this round commits to exactly that and spends the
budget on the cover screenshot and the HUD, not on a longer feature list.

## Risks

- Dev scores measure against house games, not the field; a good dev rate is
  not evidence of a final win. The target is the top-100 cutoff (99.3343%
  dev at last check), and the entry is built to be re-judged on its cover.
- The game is 2D top-down by design: it renders crisply at screenshot size
  and every system stays legible when the judge's contact sheet shrinks it.
